Journal — Venues

The rooms that keep Charleston's performers working

Every artist in our Charleston artist directory needs a stage. These are the concert halls, clubs, theaters and galleries where local talent and touring acts actually get booked.

Published Nov 17, 2026
6 min read
Charleston, SC

Downtown concert halls built for the big nights

The Charleston Gaillard Center at 95 Calhoun Street is the largest room downtown. Its 1,800-seat Martha and John M. Rivers Performance Hall is home to the Charleston Symphony Orchestra and serves as a main stage for Spoleto Festival USA, and the building also has an 11,500-square-foot exhibition hall for galas and private events.

A few blocks up, the Charleston Music Hall on John Street feels more like a listening room than an arena. It holds 965 seats inside a restored former train station, and it books touring rock, indie, comedy and tribute acts. It is also where the Charleston Jazz Orchestra plays its season, so it's a natural next stop after the jazz musicians in our directory. The College of Charleston's Sottile Theatre on George Street fills out the downtown circuit with festival programming and campus productions.

Ann Street and Morrison Drive club stages

Music Farm at 32 Ann Street is the standing-room workhorse of the peninsula, hosting live music, comedy and themed dance nights. Up on Morrison Drive, The Royal American has been central to the growth of Charleston's local music scene and is where many hometown bands play their first real gigs. Book either room for a private event and you're working with crews who already know how to light and mic a show.

Historic theaters for dance, drama and improv

The Dock Street Theatre on Church Street sits on the site of one of the first buildings in America built for theater. Today it's the home of Charleston Stage, and Spoleto uses it as a festival venue. Charleston Jazz also stages shows at Woolfe Street Playhouse, an intimate room on the upper peninsula that suits small ensembles, readings and dance showcases.

For comedy, Theatre 99 on Meeting Street has been the city's improv home for years, with The Have Nots! and a rotating roster of local troupes. Many of the circus and dance companies on our homepage rent theater space like this instead of keeping a permanent stage, so these calendars are the best way to catch them live.

Off-peninsula stages worth the drive

The Charleston Pour House on Maybank Highway opened on James Island in 2002 and has two stages. Some nights a headliner plays the indoor room while a local act plays the deck out back. On the Isle of Palms, The Windjammer at 1008 Ocean Boulevard is a long-running beach bar with live music on the weekend nights. North of town, Awendaw Green on Highway 17 hosts an outdoor barn jam that gives singer-songwriters a friendly crowd.

For arena-scale touring productions, the North Charleston Coliseum and Performing Arts Center handles Broadway tours, big concerts and family shows that need more seats than the peninsula can offer.

Gallery spaces for visual artists

Painters and printmakers need wall space, not a stage. Redux Contemporary Art Center at 1056 King Street is the anchor: it has subsidized studios, the city's only public darkroom and a rotating exhibition calendar. The City Gallery at Waterfront Park, 34 Prioleau Street, hosts juried shows like the MOJA Arts Festival exhibition, and the Cannon Street Arts Center is a city-run space that shows student and community work. The visual artists in our directory show work in these spaces regularly.
