Journal — After Dark

Charleston nightclubs where the performers are the main event

Plenty of bars play a DJ playlist. These rooms book singers, drag queens, burlesque casts and live bands, and they're the late-night side of our Charleston artist directory.

Published Nov 17, 2026
5 min read
Charleston, SC

Jazz lounges that put the band first

Forte Jazz Lounge at 477 King Street is the city's dedicated listening room. It's modeled on New York jazz clubs, with live sets every Thursday, Friday and Saturday. Owner and vocalist Joe Clarke opened it as a place where jazz is the main event rather than background music. Expect Great American Songbook nights, tribute shows and instrumental trios, with tickets sold in advance for each set.

The Commodore at 504 Meeting Street goes for a vintage supper-club look and books jazz and funk acts from around the country. Together, the two rooms carry on the lineage our jazz section traces back to the Jenkins Orphanage Band.

Drag and cabaret on Ann Street

Dudley's on Ann at 42 Ann Street has been open since 1984 and is Charleston's oldest operating gay bar. Drag shows anchor the week, with karaoke, DJs and weekend dancing filling the rest of a seven-day schedule. Each June, Dudley's teams up with neighboring Music Farm for Pride on Ann, a block party that closes the street.

Drag has spread well beyond Ann Street. Weekly drag brunches run at Well Hung Vineyard's restaurant on South Market Street, and touring celebrity-impersonation shows like Diva Royale perform dinner and brunch seatings around town. Venues label events as general audience or adults-only, so check the listing before you bring a mixed group.

Burlesque and variety nights

Burlesque in Charleston mostly runs as pop-up nights rather than at one permanent club. Sweatman's Garden on Folly Road has hosted recurring burlesque evenings with a mix of regular and new performers, and Music Farm's themed nights sometimes add variety acts. Many of the aerialists from Charleston's circus and dance community also work these cabaret bills, so following the troupes is the easiest way to find the next show.

Live-band bars that run late

For a full band after dinner, the Charleston Pour House on James Island books live music nearly every night across two stages. The Royal American on Morrison Drive is the local-band hangout, and The Windjammer handles the beach crowd on the Isle of Palms. Our guide to Charleston's performance venues covers the bigger rooms.

Bringing the show to a private party

Not every performance needs a club. Michael At Your Place books private-party and bachelorette entertainment and brings performers to rentals, hotel suites and event spaces across the Lowcountry. For the rest of the night (dinner reservations, transportation and where to go after the show), Party Charleston keeps a planning guide to the city's nightlife.
